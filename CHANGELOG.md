# Changelog

All notable changes to AskBoard are recorded here, newest first, in the layout of Keep a Changelog. Versions before 1.0.0 are 0.x releases: each ships what has merged at its release time (PRD §8).

## 0.1.0 - 2026-11-09

First release. AskBoard installs as a Claude Code plugin from a GitHub marketplace, at user or project scope, on macOS and Linux (Windows through WSL); it runs on Node 24.22.1 or later.

### Added

- Asking by pull through five tools, `ask`, `update`, `collect`, `fetch` and `snapshot`: a board of several questions and areas in the browser, answered one at a time or together with Submit, with postpone, decline, revise and follow-up, and the board's unsent work kept across a reload.
- Widgets: Choice (single and multiple), Tree (a work breakdown with optional PERT estimates, selection, bulk percentage change with undo, drag and drop) and Gantt (zoom, baseline, critical path, resizable task column, requested durations), with the answer carrying the edited values and the derived values such as the plan's finish and critical path.
- Widgets that give a result: Approval (approve, approve with changes and a required reason, reject, with a summary of the question's changes), Scale (a slider, one value or a range, or a rating) and Ranking (reorder by drag or with Move up and Move down).
- Widgets that the user edits and the answer carries as changes: LaTeX (one expression, a source field with a live preview drawn by KaTeX, and a refusal of macro definitions, KaTeX's internal commands and other commands a LaTeX item does not allow), Kanban (cards moved between columns, by drag or with "Move to...") and Timeline (milestones and ranges moved across lanes on the Gantt's time axis).
- The ready molecules: the work breakdown with PERT and the Gantt plan, linked to each other.
- The Tree asks before a work package with estimates, or one a Gantt plan names as a task, becomes a group: a drop into it, an indent under it and "Add child" on it each show a question in the toolbar row first, naming the package and what leaves the totals and the plan, with "Cancel" and "Continue"; nothing changes until "Continue" (Issue #71).
- `ask` and `update` warn when a Gantt plan names an ID that is no work package of its Tree, as a plan entry, or as a predecessor in the entry of a work package: one warning per plan, addressed to the plan, naming at most five of the IDs and counting the rest, and the formula warnings of an `ask` or `update` stay within 10,000 characters, a closing warning counting any left out; the plan stays as the agent sent it (Issue #74).
- The `askboard` skill, which tells the agent when to ask through AskBoard rather than AskUserQuestion and how to build each widget.
- The `askboard` skill teaches changing an ask (`references/updates.md`: add a question or an area; give a question a widget by a `change` of its `items`, sent whole; reopen before changing an answered question; reply to a follow-up with the question's `itemId` and the follow-up's cursor) and the ask's `language` and `locale`, and the `update` description and the server instructions name the reply's `itemId` (Issues #73, #75).
- Push delivery: answers reach a working agent without it asking, through a Claude Code mod; hooks give each ask its owner, show the board link in the terminal and keep a subagent from finishing before it has its answers; a system notification announces a new board.
- Snapshots of a board: its data and visuals, for the agent to build a document from. A Snapshot opens in the theme and density the board had when it was taken (recorded in its data), with one Theme control and the density switch (Issue #72).
- Languages (English and Italian) and settings.
- A start script, `scripts/askboard`, that runs the session process and the hooks on Node and, when Node is missing or too old, tells the agent why instead of failing silently.
- LaTeX draws its preview through KaTeX's documented interface alone (`katex.render`), after AskBoard's own scan of the source, which also counts stacked accents: an expression with 11 or more stacked combining marks on one character is refused as nested too deep. An expression is not refused for the size of its KaTeX output.
- Chart (bar, line and pie; highlighted points as the answer): the user reads a chart and highlights data points, by click or by keyboard, and the highlighted points come back as the answer's result, in the `results` of `collect` and `fetch`. The `askboard` skill teaches it.
- Atoms, the single-purpose items of a question: Text (plain or Markdown, shown as data in the sandboxed view, its `http:` and `https:` links opening in a new tab through the board page, its images as links), Value, Divider, and the input atoms TextField, NumberField, MoneyField (an amount in a currency the agent fixes or lets the user pick), DateField (with a calendar) and Toggle, each answering in the `results` of `collect` and `fetch`. An option's preview can be Markdown. Text a field cannot read stays in the field with its error and keeps "Answer" disabled until the user corrects or clears it. The `askboard` skill teaches the atoms.
- Layouts, which arrange a question's atoms and widgets: Stack (one under another), Row (two to four items side by side with relative weights, one under another when each would get less than 240 px), Group (a titled, bordered group read as a group) and Tabs (two to eight titled tabs, every panel kept mounted so a field in a hidden tab keeps its text, and a mark "needs a correction" on a tab whose panel holds a field to correct), up to four deep. A nested item answers in the `results` of `collect` and `fetch` under its own ID, and `fetch` of a layout returns the items inside it; Snapshots show them. Item IDs are unique at every depth of the ask. Excerpts, references, images and molecule references stay in the question's own items. The `askboard` skill teaches the layouts.
- Table, an editable grid for a question: columns of text, number, money (one currency to a column), date and on/off, each with the bounds of its input atom; totals and formula columns computed from the board's graph (a formula reads its own row as `[@field]` and other items one at a time); the user edits cells, adds a row and deletes one, from the keyboard or the pointer, and text a cell cannot read stays in the cell and keeps "Answer" from being sent, across a reload too. The edits come back as the answer's `changes` and the computed cells and totals as its derived values. The `askboard` skill teaches it.
- The ID check of `update`: a deleted question's top-level item IDs stay refused (a later `add` cannot reuse them) and `fetch` of one answers `deleted`; a molecule reference's own ID (`wbs`) is held against plain items in every question, the reference's own included, before or after its question is deleted; the IDs it expands to are held like any item's, and a refusal names the reference's ID.
- `fetch` of an item the agent deleted (a question, an area, an item inside one, or a molecule reference of one) returns the outcome `deleted`, naming the ask and the item; an ID a molecule reference expanded to answers `missing`, and an item a `change` dropped or one never held is an error (Issue #73).
- An ID a molecule reference of the ask has expanded to stays held against items for the ask's life: `update` refuses a plain item, a layout's items or tabs, or a reference that would take it, as in use, naming the reference (Issues #73, #75).
- The unresolved-pointer warnings of `ask` and `update` stay within 10,000 characters, a closing warning counting any left out, so an `ask` result stays within 30,000 characters whatever the pointers an agent sends (Issue #98).
- Forms: a question's input atoms, arranged by layouts, make a form. Each input atom can be `required` (its label ends with "(required)", and it shows no error while empty); the TextField takes `minLength`, `maxLength` and a `pattern`, and a new PickField picks one option or several from a list of 2 to 50 (radios or checkboxes, never a dropdown, with a filter above 10 options and `minItems` and `maxItems` for several). A field that fails its check shows its error under it, keeps its value, and keeps "Answer" (and "Accept recommended", "Select recommended" and Submit) disabled with a hint naming the field; `ask-core` also refuses an answer that fails a check, except a `pattern`, which runs only on the board. A PickField answers in the `results` of `collect` and `fetch` as an option ID or a list of them. The `askboard` skill teaches the Form.
- Document review, for a document the user reads and marks: the agent sends Markdown (up to 10,000 characters, with an optional title), the user selects a passage and comments on it or suggests its replacement (an empty replacement suggests deleting it), with a numbered pin on each passage, a card that writes into the draft as the user types, a counter, and a path through the document's blocks and pins from the keyboard; the answer's `results` give each annotation with the quoted text and up to 200 characters of context, and a suggested edit's `replacement`, in `collect` and `fetch`. After the agent changes the document, a comment whose quote no longer occurs once is listed as detached and "Answer" waits until the user deletes it; an annotation is never dropped or moved to another passage on a guess. The links of the document open through the board page, in a Snapshot the document is read-only with its marks and pins, and the `askboard` skill teaches it. The Text and the Document review share one Markdown renderer, a library chunk.
- After "Keep mine", "Answer", "Accept recommended", "Select recommended" and Submit read each field at the revision the card shows and the answer names, as the card's own state and hint do, so a press never refuses what the card shows as ready; what the answer carries, the draft and the items each widget reads follow that revision too, and when the agent's revision adds an item to a question holding work, the card offers "Keep mine" and "Reload, losing my work" (#92, #93, #100). A widget item that fails in the view stops every act of its question, Submit and the board page's checks included, except the choice to reload (#103); in submitted delivery Submit sends the other questions.
- A question the agent reopens starts from the user's previous answer when the agent changed nothing else in it and every part of the answer still fits, and is filled once (a reload or a takeover does not fill it again over what the user undid, and a closed ask is never filled): its choice, notes, results and the edits to its widgets (a moved Timeline entry, a Kanban card, a Gantt task) are in the draft, nothing is sent, and a line under "Reopened by the agent" says so. Otherwise it starts from the agent's version, and the line says that and points to "Start from my previous answer". The board state says which items an answer on an earlier revision shares with the current one, and the draft record marks a revision the board filled (Issue #70).
- "Start from my previous answer" and the first edit of an answered question bring back the edits to the question's widgets, except those of an item the agent has changed since: an answer carries what the card shows, so a revised answer carries an edited widget the user left alone (Issue #70).
- A revision shows what it sends: a result the revision's props no longer take is neither shown nor sent, and a result the user clears, or a value the user returns to the agent's version, stays cleared, on screen and in the answer (Issues #105, #107).

### Known limits

- The repository is private: installing needs read access and git credentials.
- No automatic updates: a third-party marketplace does not update by itself (see the README).
- LaTeX, K-1: a nesting form the scan does not count, unknown today or added by a KaTeX release, can reach Chromium's crash depth (about 130 levels) and crash the board's tab, on every load if the agent sent it. The scan counts every form known on KaTeX 0.19.1, and its limit is about 12 times under the crash.
- LaTeX, K-2: a very wide expression, such as about 2,000 empty matrix cells, drew in 118 ms in Chromium and 415 ms in WebKit (measured on an M3 Max), and may pass the 2-second rule on slow hardware; no realistic formula comes near.
- The Bun executable form, `askboard doctor`, the light benchmark and the remaining widgets and molecules of the PRD come in later releases.
- A Markdown Text of the densest table (100 columns by 190 short rows, about 19,000 elements, 783 code points of source) renders in under 100 ms, but an ask of 200 such Texts is ready after about 17 s in Chromium and about 53 s in WebKit (an ask of 200 Texts at the 200,000-character cap takes about 1.0 s and 1.4 s more than a plain board). Nothing bounds the number of Texts in an ask; a pathological ask of this kind is handled later as a bug.
- Forms, pattern timing: a `pattern` is held to a safe subset (no backreference, lookaround, nested repetition or script property; at most 3 repetitions) and the text of a field with a pattern holds at most 100 characters, a limit the user can see, since the pattern runs on the board in the user's browser, at most once per keystroke, for the field that changed, whatever the number of fields (each field keeps its last outcome and its compiled pattern with its own props object). The worst case measured (a class uniting several property escapes as the head, over a run of astral characters at 100 characters) took about 341 ms on Node 24 (V8, Chromium's engine) and under 5 ms on Bun (Safari's engine), on one machine; every case stays under 500 ms. A pattern is not run by the session process.
- Table, K-1: the bound on its cost is per item. A board of many Tables (or of many Trees) is not bounded as a whole: each Table holds at most 200 rows and 10 columns, and nothing limits how many an ask holds.
- Table, K-2: a date cell is typed in the ask's date format; it has no calendar (the DateField has one).
- Table, K-3: rows and columns cannot be reordered, and a Table cannot be sorted or filtered.
- Table, K-4: a 200-row Table whose every formula reads a total can take just over 100 ms to show an edit in WebKit (98 to 105 ms measured, of which 4 to 5 ms in the graph, the rest the paint of 200 rows); its gate there is 150 ms. An edit of a Table whose formulas read their own row takes 88 to 93 ms in WebKit.
- Table, K-5: the figures of the worst Table (200 rows, 10 columns, nine formula columns of 500 characters, all totalled) that came near their gates of design decision 13. In a scratch probe with no parse cache, build and first evaluation took 134 to 141 ms on Node 24 (gate 150 ms; 110 to 116 ms on Bun) and a rebuild with a 201st row 113 to 122 ms on Node; the built code takes 73 to 80 ms on Node and 50 to 56 ms on Bun. Under heavy load on the machine the built code's build took 142.1 ms on Bun and 152.9 ms on Node in the first runs, the latter 2.9 ms over the gate. A cell edit painted in WebKit in 88 to 93 ms for the worst form (gate 100 ms); the board with it visible took 271 to 294 ms (gate 1,500 ms) and a row added 61 to 70 ms (gate 250 ms).
- Size: an answer, or one Submit, goes to the board as one message of at most about 6 MB, and a board's unsent work is kept across a reload up to about 2 million characters. Past the first, the card says "Not sent: this answer is too large" with its size and the limit, sends nothing and keeps your work (a Submit says so on each question in it and points to immediate delivery, one question at a time); past the second, the card of the question holding the largest work says that a reload would lose it, the leave-page prompt stays armed and "Copy my unsent work" still holds it. The agent receives only what you sent.
- Document review, quotes: a quote gives the text as the board shows it, with Markdown marks removed (a passage of `**bold**` is quoted as `bold`), so the agent finds the passage in its own source or asks; a passage that still occurs more than once with 200 characters of context is refused when the user selects it, and a block over 2,000 characters is not commented from the keyboard (a message beside the toolbar, not a card, says to select part of it with the pointer).
- Document review, cost: placing 100 near-miss quotes of 2,000 characters (quotes the text does not hold, over a document of one repeated character) took about 200 ms in Bun (419 ms for another form) and the board showed such a review in 173 ms in Chromium, both over 50 ms; a Document review holds at most 100 annotations, but nothing bounds how many an ask holds (32 reviews with 3 such quotes each were shown in 375 ms in Chromium).
- Document review, size: two Document reviews of one question filled to their bounds in emoji give a draft record of 3,384,911 characters against 2,000,000 and an `answer` act of 6,744,897 bytes against the board's message limit of 6,004,096: the host refuses the act whole, the card says "Not sent: this answer is too large" with the size and the limit and keeps the user's work, and the draft is mirrored without that question's work, the card saying that a reload would lose it.
- Document review, after a reload or a takeover under kept work: while a card shows another revision than the one the user's work names, the Document review is read-only with one line saying why, its held comments are still sent naming the kept revision, and an annotation already detached on that revision is sent with them; it becomes editable when no work remains on the kept revision, or when the user chooses "Reload, losing my work". After a reload, work held only on an item that the agent's revision removed is not restored.
